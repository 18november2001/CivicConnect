Person 1: Scope, Stakeholder and Value Engineering 

1. Problem and Business Need 

1.1 Problem Statement 

The main problem that CivicConnect is trying to solve is the way service requests are currently being handled. Now, requests can come through different channels such as WhatsApp, emails, telephone calls, spreadsheets and paper records. Having information spread across so many different places makes it difficult for the organization to properly keep track of every request. 

This can cause several problems. A request could be missed, duplicated, sent to the wrong person or even lost. Requesters may also not know what is happening after they have submitted something. Staff can struggle to see who is responsible for a request or which requests still need attention. Management also does not always have reliable information to see what is still open, overdue or already completed. 

CivicConnect therefore needs to bring this process into one system where service requests can be properly recorded and tracked from the time, they are submitted until they are resolved or closed. 

1.2 Business Need 

The organization needs a better and more organized way of handling service requests. CivicConnect will provide one digital platform where requests can be submitted, managed, monitored and reported. 

For requesters, this means they should be able to submit a request and later check what is happening with it. For staff, it should make it easier to see the requests they are responsible for, update them and record the work that has been done. Management should also be able to get useful information about service requests without having to manually collect information from different places. 

The goal is not just to replace WhatsApp, emails or spreadsheets with another system. The real value of CivicConnect is having one controlled and traceable process that makes it easier to see what has happened to a request and who is responsible for it.

  2.2 Requesters 

Requesters are the people who submit service requests. 

Their main concern is being able to submit a request without making the process unnecessarily difficult. After submitting it, they should also be able to see what is happening with it instead of having to contact staff repeatedly for an update. 

They should be able to view the status of their submitted requests; see a history of requests they previously submitted and receive useful feedback when something changes. 

This makes requesters a high,interest stakeholder because the system directly affects how they report problems and receive feedback. 

2.3 Staff 

Staff are responsible for dealing with the requests that have been submitted. 

They need to be able to see the requests that are relevant to them and view enough information to understand what needs to be done. They also need ways to search, filter and sort requests so that they can find the information they need. 

Staff should also be able to accept or be assigned responsibility for a request, update their status and record comments, actions or resolution information. 

Staff have both high influence and high interest because CivicConnect will directly affect how they manage their daily service requests. 

2.4 Management / Oversight 

Management needs a broader view of what is happening across the service, request process. 

They need to know how many requests still open, which ones are overdue and which have already been resolved or closed. They should also be able to view information based on things such as category and status. 

This information is important because management needs to understand how services are performed and whether requests are being dealt with properly. 

2.5 Organization 

The organization has an interest in CivicConnect because the current fragmented process makes it difficult to maintain reliable records and accountability. 

The organisation needs CivicConnect to provide a more organised way of handling requests without creating a system that becomes too expensive or difficult to operate. 

2.6 Project Team 

The project team is also an important stakeholder because the team is responsible for delivering CivicConnect. 

The team needs to make sure that the project does not become too large to complete. Adding more features may sound useful, but every additional feature also creates more work in areas such as requirements, design, development, security and testing. 

The Master Project Brief specifically warns that additional features create more engineering obligations, and that a smaller, controlled solution can be stronger than a larger unfinished one.

4. Competing Stakeholder Needs 

One challenge with CivicConnect is that different stakeholders can want different things. Sometimes one stakeholder's expectation can also make another part of the system more difficult. These differences need to be considered when deciding what should be included in the project. 

4.1 Requester Visibility and Security 

Requesters want to know what is happening with their requests. This means CivicConnect needs to give them enough information to see the progress of something they have submitted. 

At the same time, the system may contain information that should not be visible to everyone. Giving users too much access could create security and privacy problems. 

For this reason, requester status and history are included in the scope, but access to information still needs to be controlled. 

4.2 Simple Submission and Enough Information 

Requesters will probably prefer a short and simple process when submitting a service request. Staff, however, need enough information to understand the problem and decide what needs to happen next. 

Making the submission form too complicated could make the system frustrating to use. On the other hand, collecting too little information could make the request difficult for staff to process. 

The system therefore needs to find a balance by collecting the necessary information and using controlled categories without making submission unnecessarily complicated. 

  

  

4.3 Management Reporting and Project Size 

Management could benefit from detailed reports and advanced analytics. However, building a large reporting system would increase the amount of work required by the project team. 

The initial scope will therefore focus on the management information required by the project, such as identifying open, overdue, resolved and closed requests and viewing information by category or status. 

More advanced analytics can be considered later. 

4.4 More Features and Available Time 

It is possible that stakeholders may suggest additional features during the project. Some of these ideas could be useful but accepting everything would make the project difficult to control. 

The project brief makes it clear that committed scope needs to be baselined and controlled and that uncontrolled scope creep is not acceptable.  

The team should therefore focus on the important CivicConnect functionality first and only add other features when their value and impact have been properly considered. 

  

5. Scope Baseline 

5.1 Scope Statement 

CivicConnect will be a digital platform used to manage community service requests from the point where a request is submitted until it is resolved or closed. 

The system will allow requesters to submit and track their requests. Staff will be able to view and manage the requests they are responsible for, while management will be able to view useful information about service activity. 

The focus of the project is to deliver the minimum business capabilities required for CivicConnect while keeping the scope realistic and manageable.

8. Deliberate Feature Deferral 

AI, Assisted Request Categorization 

One feature that we decided to defer is AI, assisted request categorization. 

CivicConnect already needs a controlled way for requests to be placed into categories. The project brief requires a controlled category mechanism but does not say that AI needs to be used for this.  

Using AI could possibly make categorization quicker in the future, but there are still questions that would need to be answered. For example, the AI could place a request in the wrong category, and the team would need to decide who checks or corrects that decision. There could also be security, privacy and cost concerns depending on how the AI service works. 

For these reasons, we decided that it is better to first build a reliable normal categorization process. AI,assisted categorization can be looked at again later if there is a clear reason for including it. 

  

9. Scope Control 

Once the team agrees on the scope and the Milestone 1 baseline is approved, new features should not just be added without considering what effect they will have on the project. 

For example, if a stakeholder asks for a new feature, the team will need to look at whether it changes the requirements, security, design, data, testing, schedule, cost or resources. 

The Master Project Brief uses the following process for changes after baseline: 

Change Request → Impact Analysis → Decision → Authorized → Implement → Verify → Update Baseline.  

Using this process will help the team avoid uncontrolled scope creep. 

  

10. Conclusion 

CivicConnect is being developed to give the organization a better way of handling community service requests. Instead of having requests spread across different channels, the aim is to have one controlled system where requests can be submitted, tracked, managed and completed. 

The stakeholder analysis shows that requesters, staff and management have different needs. Requesters mainly need visibility and feedback, staff need a clear way to manage requests, and management needs reliable information about service activity. 

The scope was therefore kept focused on the main functionality needed by these stakeholders. Other features that could make the project unnecessarily large or complicated have either been left out or moved to future scope. 

Having these boundaries in place gives the team a clearer idea of what needs to be delivered and helps prevent the project from growing beyond what can realistically be completed.
